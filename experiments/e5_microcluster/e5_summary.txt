   EXPERIMENT 9F (E5): MICRO-CLUSTERING ABLATION REPORT                         
================================================================================

SCIENTIFIC HYPOTHESIS:
  Can reducing the local density requirement to min_cluster_size=3 recover sparse
  emerging-topic seeds (such as FDA/Pfizer vaccine mandates) that are invisible to
  standard clustering (N >= 7), while measuring the trade-off in candidate noise?

SUMMARY OF KEY FINDINGS:
  1. High Sensitivity Gain (Sparse Seed Recovery):
     - Under E4 (min_cluster_size=7): Exactly ZERO vaccine or FDA clusters formed.
     - Under E5 (min_cluster_size=3): 10 discrete vaccine clusters formed!
       * D_16 (TRAJ_573, 3 posts, Coherence: 0.938): Healthcare workers and vaccine mandates.
       * D_18 (TRAJ_661, 3 posts, Coherence: 0.792): Delta Airlines employee vaccine surcharge.
       * D_26 (TRAJ_961, 3 posts, Coherence: 0.829): School/workplace vaccine requirements.
       * D_10 (TRAJ_345, 6 posts, Coherence: 0.754): "Get vaccinated or lose your job?" [BURST].
  
  2. The Noise Explosion Trade-off:
     - Total Trajectories exploded from 132 to 1,121 (8.5x increase).
     - Validated Candidates surged from 35 to 141 (4.0x increase).
     - Candidate Micro-Seeds generated: 721.
     - High sensitivity comes at a substantial precision cost: tiny clusters of 3-4 conversational
       posts readily achieve high cosine similarity due to small sample size.
  
  3. Cluster Fragmentation of Medium-Sized Topics:
     - OnlyFans was captured at D_11 (TRAJ_406, 9 posts, 3.0% share, coherence 0.710),
       though slightly fragmented from its original 11 posts.
     - Hurricane Ida was fragmented from a 7-post cluster into a 3-post [MICRO_SEED] (TRAJ_981, coherence: 0.802).
  
  4. The Boundary of Micro-Clustering (Reddit Locked Comments):
     - Reddit locked comments mentions remained below N=3 in any single checkpoint
       (D_22: 1 post, D_25: 1 post, D_27: 2 posts).
     - Therefore, micro-clustering at N=3 still cannot cluster 1-2 stochastic posts.
